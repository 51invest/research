Frequency and Time Alignment

Series at different frequencies must first share a reference period and an information set; aggregation is usually safer than disaggregation, and release times and time zones can change the date.

ModuleRES-M04 · 数据口径
LevelFoundation
Read6 min · 12 sections

This article has an interactive demo ↓

Research question

Monthly indicators, quarterly GDP and annual benchmarks often share a chart or regression, yet their reference periods, release times and revisions differ. This article asks how to align them to a common period, when values can be added and when they can only be estimated, and how publication lags and time zones affect what was available at a given date. All numbers, dates and zones are hypothetical.

Data

Alignment starts by stating each series’ reference period, frequency and release schedule. The table uses three hypothetical series with consistent coverage and definitions, all recorded as unadjusted source values with amounts in renminbi: a monthly flow, a quarterly flow and an annual benchmark.

Benchmarking keeps series of the same target variable coherent across frequencies, interpolation estimates intermediate values between known values, temporal distribution breaks a low-frequency benchmark into higher-frequency observations, and denoting is a special case of temporal distribution — the Statistics Canada glossary definitions [1].

Method and assumptions

Choose the target frequency and common sample, then the direction. High-to-low is usually aggregation: flows sum, stocks take the end-of-period value, and rate and price series such as a daily interest rate neither sum nor uniformly take the closing value — they are averaged with a stated weighting or taken as the last available observation of the period, and an unweighted mean of daily observations is itself a weighting choice. Indices need weights rather than addition. Low-to-high is disaggregation: interpolation passes through known points, while temporal distribution also requires high-frequency values to sum to the benchmark. Both add assumptions, so label output as estimates, not observations.

Separate the reference period from the release time. A lag usually means the current period is unavailable at period end; a backtest may use only the vintage published then, not later revisions. Timestamps need a zone or UTC offset: one instant can fall on different local dates. A common period can be an intersection or union, but missing values in a union must stay flagged, never zero; dropping the gap period from the common sample is also a valid choice, and whether it was dropped or estimated must be stated.

Release arrangements are themselves part of the conditions of data use: the IMF’s SDDS lists data coverage, periodicity and timeliness, advance release calendars and public access among its dissemination dimensions [2].

Results

Monthly flows of 100, 110 and 120 give a first-quarter total of 100 + 110 + 120 = 330, matching the hypothetical quarterly value of 330. The second quarter is 340; the quarters 330, 340, 350 and 360 sum to 1380, matching the annual benchmark. If March is missing but the first quarter is 330, the implied March value is 120; it is derived, not observed. Without the quarterly value, carrying 110 forward gives 320, an error of 10.

An annual value of 1380 spread evenly gives 1380 / 4 = 345 per quarter. The total is preserved, but no quarter is observed, and smoother interpolation cannot remove the uncertainty; report the method and an interval rather than treating 345 as fact.

Release example: quarterly data are published 60 days after the quarter and monthly data after 30 days. On 15 April, first-quarter GDP is unavailable while January and February are; using it would be future information. Zone example: 2026-04-15 09:00 UTC+8 is 01:00 UTC, which is 2026-04-14 21:00 at UTC-4; record the instant and the offset.

Robustness

Compare aggregation against the benchmark and interpolation or distribution against the same benchmark, reporting differences rather than one result. Repeat with real-time and latest-revision vintages to see whether the conclusions depend on revisions. Change the target frequency, sample window and period endpoint (end, midpoint or average). Check trading days, leap years, Easter and 4-4-5 calendars, which can make a month or quarter non-comparable, and daylight saving, which shifts the local UTC offset.

Limitations

Disaggregation depends on the model and indicator chosen and may smooth away turning points; lags and revisions create look-ahead bias; a missing zone can shift a date by one day; changes in definitions, coverage and seasonal adjustment break comparability. Alignment does not identify causation or repair selection and measurement error.

Two cases come from this project's own registers and are real handling choices, not the teaching assumptions above. First, docs/dataset-candidates.json substitutes the World Bank Pink Sheet monthly WTI nominal price (WTI_MONTHLY) for the originally planned EIA daily spot series, because the EIA interface needs a key this environment does not have; that change turns the researchable question from daily volatility into monthly levels and ranges, so frequency is not a free parameter. Second, CHART-010 in docs/chart-register.json, the five-economy year-on-year CPI／HICP panel, spans 2024-01 to 2025-12, keeping 23 observations after the month with no U.S. value is dropped, and the common window stops at 2025-12 because the euro-area HICP annual-rate series ends there ahead of the 2026 methodology change. The end of one series truncates the whole common sample, and that truncation has to appear in the caption and the notes rather than being papered over by extending the other series.

The time reference follows the NIST description: UTC is displayed on a 24-hour clock and is the official U.S. time reference, with U.S. time zones expressed as offsets from UTC, for example EST as UTC−5 and PST as UTC−8 [3].

Reproduction

Record the target frequency, aggregation function, interpolation method and constraints, sample window, vintage, release instant with UTC offset, seasonal-adjustment status and revision policy; keep raw data and code; label values as observed, aggregated or estimated.

Strength of conclusions

Alignment and aggregation are descriptive; interpolation and temporal distribution are model outputs, not observations. Given the benchmark and assumptions, the quarterly total is 330; March need not have been 120 in reality. Correlation after alignment is not causation.

Exercises

  1. Monthly flows are 100, 110 and 120, and the quarterly value is 330. What is the quarterly total? If the monthly series were an index, could the values be added?
  2. The annual benchmark is 1380 and is spread evenly over four quarters. What is each quarter, and can this recover the true quarterly path?
  3. A release occurs at 2026-04-15 09:00 UTC+8. What is the UTC instant, and what is the local date at UTC-4?
Suggested answers
  1. The total is 330, consistent with the quarterly value. An index level cannot be added; convert it to a flow or growth rate first.
  2. Each quarter is 1380 / 4 = 345; only the total is preserved, so the true path cannot be recovered and 345 is an estimate.
  3. The UTC instant is 2026-04-15 01:00; at UTC-4 the local time is 2026-04-14 21:00, so the local date is 14 April.
